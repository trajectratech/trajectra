# Brand strategy

## Current positioning, read from the site

> "Trajectra Technologies is a forward-thinking software company specializing in
> custom software development, technical training, and IT consulting… Driven by
> innovation. Powered by expertise. Your trajectory starts here."

This is a **category description, not a position.** Replace "Trajectra" with any
of ten thousand agency names and it still reads correctly — which is the
definition of a failed positioning statement.

### Strengths

- **The name is genuinely good.** "Trajectra" implies trajectory, motion,
  direction. It is short, ownable, pronounceable in English, and the `.com`
  is held. That is a real asset most agencies do not have.
- **Legitimate CAC registration** — a verifiable fact that is currently
  mentioned nowhere.
- **A free tool already exists.** Most agencies never ship anything of their own.
- **Genuine geographic advantage.** Lagos is UTC+1: four hours of overlap with
  London, six with New York, and full overlap with continental Europe. That is
  materially better than the Asian outsourcing markets Western buyers default
  to, and the site does not mention it once.

### Weaknesses

- **Six services, no focus.** Custom software, training, advisory, maintenance,
  *computer networking*, and digital transformation. Networking sits beside
  software development and signals a general IT shop rather than a product
  engineering firm — it lowers perceived value on everything else.
- **Zero proof.** No clients, cases, testimonials, numbers, or named people.
- **Adjective-driven copy.** "World-class", "cutting-edge", "forward-thinking",
  "elite". Unsupported superlatives read as weakness to a technical buyer.
- **Two brand names in circulation** — "Trajectra" and "Trajectra Technologies"
  — plus `trajectratech.youcanbook.me` as a third. This directly caused the
  Google site-name problem.
- **The one location statement is inward-facing.** "Empowering African tech
  talent" describes what Trajectra does *for Africa*. It says nothing about what
  a buyer in Munich gets.
- **A fabricated 4.9-star rating in the markup.** Removed. If that shipped to a
  prospect who inspected the source, the credibility cost would be total.

### Opportunities

- **"African tech talent" is an asset, not a liability — if reframed.** Buyers
  are actively looking for alternatives to saturated markets. The story is
  "European-overlap engineering at a rational rate," not "we are from Africa."
- **Radical transparency as differentiation.** Publish the process, the
  timelines and the contract terms. Nearly no agency does. It is the cheapest
  and fastest trust mechanism available, and it works without publishing a
  single figure.
- **The tools directory as a brand engine.** Free tools that engineers and
  designers actually use build more credibility per dollar than any advertising.

## Recommended positioning

> **Trajectra is a product engineering partner for companies that need to ship —
> senior teams in the European timezone, at a rate that makes sense.**

Three components, each defensible:

1. **Product engineering partner** — not "software company", not "IT services".
   Signals ownership of outcomes rather than hours.
2. **Companies that need to ship** — the buyer is a funded startup or a
   mid-market firm with a stalled roadmap. Not "businesses and individuals".
3. **European timezone at a rational rate** — the concrete, checkable advantage.

### What this requires you to drop

Positioning is subtraction. To be *the* company that ships product, Trajectra
should stop leading with:

- **Computer networking and design.** Different buyer, different sales cycle,
  no synergy with product engineering. Keep serving existing clients; remove it
  from the homepage.
- **"Advisory Role"** as a headline service. It is not a category anyone
  searches for. Fold it into the delivery engagements.
- **Technical training as a co-equal pillar.** It is a good business but a
  different one — B2B/B2C education, different funnel, different price point.
  Give it its own page (and eventually its own sub-brand) rather than a third
  of the homepage.

That leaves three services on the homepage: **build a product**, **fix / modernise
an existing one**, **embed a team**. Everything else lives one level down.

### Homepage hero

**Headline:** Custom software teams for companies that need to ship.
**Sub:** Trajectra builds, modernises and staffs product engineering — senior
engineers on European hours, from Lagos.
**Primary CTA:** Book a 30-minute call
**Secondary:** See our work

### Service descriptions — the pattern

Every service should answer four questions in this order: *what you get*,
*who it is for*, *how long*, *on what terms*.

> **MVP development**
> A working, production-deployed product in 8–12 weeks — not a prototype.
> Web app, API, infrastructure, CI, and a handover that lets your team take over.
> **Best for:** pre-seed and seed startups with a validated idea and no team.
> **Typical:** 10 weeks · fixed scope · fixed price agreed before we start ·
> IP yours on payment.

Note the fourth line does the work a price would have done. "Fixed price agreed
before we start" answers the buyer's real fear — an open-ended bill — without
quoting a number.

Compare with the current text for the same service — 60 words containing
"tailored", "scalable", "innovative", "high-performance", "boost efficiency"
and "drive digital transformation", and not one concrete fact.

## Building trust with international clients

The buyer's real question is "what happens if this goes wrong?" Answer it before
they ask:

1. **Named people with faces.** The highest-leverage change available. Anonymity
   is the single largest trust cost.
2. **Publish the CAC registration number** and founding year in the footer.
3. **Publish the commercial model, if not the number** — fixed-scope projects
   vs. monthly retainers, minimum engagement length, what a fixed price covers
   and what triggers a change order. See below.
4. **Publish the contract terms** — IP assigned on payment, source in the
   client's repository from day one, no lock-in, 30-day exit. Put this on
   `/how-we-work`.
5. **State the timezone as a headline benefit,** not a footnote.
6. **Third-party verification:** Clutch profile with real reviews, GitHub
   organisation with public work, LinkedIn company page with real employees.
7. **A written engagement process.** Four steps, plainly stated, from first call
   to handover.
8. **Payment terms in a currency they use.** Invoicing in USD/EUR with Wise or
   Stripe removes a practical objection nobody voices but everybody has.
9. **One paying reference client willing to take a call.** Worth more than
   everything else on this list combined.

## Qualifying without publishing prices

You have decided not to publish figures (2026-08-01). That is a legitimate and
common choice — it keeps pricing flexible per client and avoids anchoring
against a number a prospect sees out of context. The cost is real though: more
discovery calls that go nowhere, and a buyer who cannot self-qualify.

Almost all of the benefit of a price page is recoverable without a number:

1. **Publish the engagement model.** "We work in fixed-scope projects of 8 weeks
   or more, and monthly retainers with a three-month minimum." A prospect
   looking for ten hours of work now disqualifies themselves.
2. **Publish durations.** "MVP Sprint — 10 weeks." Duration correlates closely
   enough with cost that a serious buyer can estimate the order of magnitude,
   and it makes the offer concrete.
3. **Put a budget-range question on the booking form.** Four bands, required
   field. This is the single most effective substitute for a price page and
   takes ten minutes to add.
4. **Say what a fixed price covers** and what triggers a change order. The fear
   is not the price; it is the price changing.
5. **Publish the contract terms** — IP on payment, code in the client's repo
   from day one, 30-day exit. This is where the trust actually comes from.

Revisit the decision once there are 3–5 case studies live. Published prices work
best alongside proof; published prices *without* proof invite the wrong
comparison.

## Brand consistency checklist

- Site name, `og:site_name`, `WebSite.name`: **Trajectra** (done).
- Legal name **Trajectra Technologies** only in legal, contractual and
  `legalName` contexts (done).
- Every social profile display name: **Trajectra**. Currently inconsistent —
  fix manually on each platform.
- Move booking from `trajectratech.youcanbook.me` to `cal.trajectra.com` or
  `trajectra.com/book`. A third-party domain in the primary CTA leaks brand
  equity and looks improvised at exactly the moment the buyer is deciding.
